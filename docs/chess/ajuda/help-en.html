1Overview

PdfCraft reads a chess book in PDF. It detects the diagrams and reads each one as a position (a FEN), and it reads the moves in the text, figurines and annotation signs included. With a book read, you can:

  • review the positions read and correct the wrong ones, without changing the PDF;
  • study any diagram or game of the book on an analysis board, with variations, comments, drawings and an engine such as Stockfish;
  • open a printed move on the board by double-clicking it on the page, and transcribe whole passages by selecting their text;
  • align the book to the PGN that comes with its digital edition (Chessable, Forward Chess…);
  • see where in the book the board's position appears, or a given material and pawn structure;
  • export the book as PGN, EPUB, HTML, Word, RTF or Markdown, and its exercises as a sheet to print, a PGN of problems or Anki cards.

Everything runs on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Notices and errors

Every chess message shows at the foot of the page area, and a screen reader says it. A notice goes at your next click or key; when what was done can be undone, it carries Undo (confirming or forgetting a correction, redrawing in the PDF, putting a lichess link, keeping the book's PGN, aligning the book). What failed (a file not written or not read, the book's PGN or the corrections not kept, games not autosaved, a model that couldn't be loaded) is a red error with ×, which stays until you dismiss it. Esc dismisses it while the keyboard is in the chess panels or on the error itself; errors of the same moment are kept together (“… (and 2 more)”), and doing again with success what failed takes its error away. An internal error in a long job is said too (“The work stopped on an internal error: …”), instead of leaving the panel busy.

PdfCraft's chess edition is a folder, with no installer: dist/app, holding the program (pdfcraft.exe), the command line (pdfcraft-cli.exe) and three folders beside them. None of the files below comes with PdfCraft's code; without them, everything else keeps working.

3Detecting and reviewing the diagrams

Reviewing a book in 5 steps

  1. Detect. Click Detect diagrams at the top of the Diagrams tab (or in the Chess menu). Its ▾ (Where to detect) offers the whole book (In the whole book (4 pages)) or On this page. The progress bar says the page detection has reached (Detecting… page 75 of 703), and Stop stops it between two pages; the diagrams read so far stay.
  2. Review. At the end, the notice at the foot of the page sums up, for instance “4 diagrams · 1 to review”, with Review now, which opens the first diagram to review in the editor and sets the list's filter back to All (4).
  3. Correct. Put the pieces where the book has them: a click on a square puts the palette's piece there, or use the keyboard. The footer says at once whether the position is legal and, when it isn't, why.
  4. Confirm. Confirm and next (Ctrl+Enter). The correction is kept, without changing the PDF, and the next diagram to review opens. A reading that was right only needs Ctrl+Enter. After the last one, the editor says Review done: 1 of 1 checked.
  5. Export. Export book… writes the book with your corrections (chapter 18).

At the top of the tab are Detect diagrams ▾, Export book… (the file icon), Board and the ⋯ menu, More chess actions. In a narrow column Board keeps only its icon and Detect diagrams is cut short with “…”; the whole name is in the tooltip.

On the desktop, detection uses several cores (up to four, leaving one for the rest of the program): the list fills in by batches of pages, in the book's order. On the web it reads one page at a time.

Each open book has its own list. Detecting in another book never empties this one's, and while another book is being detected, a book with no detection of its own says Detecting in another book… page 306 of 748 under its header. The page read in the Text tab, the list's filter, the export's choices and the book origin also belong to each book.

Opening a book again does not read it all again

What detection read of each page (and what the chess OCR read of a scanned page) is kept in the chess\reading folder, up to 512 MiB in all, the books used longest ago leaving first; nothing is kept while the disk has less than 1 GB free. When you open the book again, the list comes back without reading the pages. A page is read again when it changed, or with another piece model, other settings, or a newer version's reading. A book that does not allow copying keeps no reading at all, and an encrypted one only after Keep them in the question about your chess work (the reading kept is not encrypted). An edit of the PDF, or Undo and Redo, keeps the list: only the pages that changed are read again, in the background.

The states of a diagram

Every diagram has a state, shown by an icon and a word, never by colour alone: in the list, on its mark on the page and in the editor. The list's row has a short word (Corrected, PGN; with unconfirmed changes, just Not confirmed, with a dot on the icon); the whole state is in the tooltip and in what a screen reader says.

Who is to move, and the move number

The side to move comes from what the page says, in this order:

  • the diagram's caption, or a heading over the page's diagrams, in words (“Black to move”, “White to play and win”, “Pretas jogam”, “Weiß am Zug”, “Black to move in all positions”) — only short lines with no moves, never the analysis;
  • the first bold move number under the diagram, in the same column and before the next diagram: 23.♘f3 says White is to move, and 23...♖xe4 that Black is, at move 23 (a number in the body font is prose and is passed over);
  • a “Mate in 2” problem (#2, “Mate em 2”, up to three moves): when the page doesn't say who is to move, the side that mates is, if only one of them does.

When the position itself contradicts the side printed (the side not to move would be in check), the position wins, and the diagram goes to review. With none of these, the side is assumed: White is to move, and the exports say “side to move not given”.

The Diagrams list

The filters (chips) show the groups that have diagrams, with their count: All (4), Read (3), To review (1) and, when there are any, Not read (n), With an error (n), Verified (n), Corrected (n) and Confirmed by the PGN (n). One applies at a time, plus On this page. Follow the page keeps the page on screen at the top of the list.

The diagrams are grouped under their page's header, with the number the book prints (pages in Roman numerals, or a book that starts at page 25, say “xvii” and “25”). The header's menu has Mark the page as checked: every diagram of that page read with confidence becomes Verified, in one step that Undo takes back. Those to review are left for you: accepting them in bulk is deliberately not offered.

Each row shows the diagram and its state; the FEN is in the tooltip. ↑ ↓ Home End walk the rows, Enter opens the editor with the keyboard on its board, and Esc, in the editor, goes back to the row.

On the page, each diagram has a mark in its state's colour, its number on a tab; a click picks the diagram, with the Select tool or the Hand. A double-click on a diagram on the page opens it on the board.

Each row's ⋯ menu

  • Open on the board: puts the position on the analysis board, as a new game.
  • Analyze: opens it on the board with the engine analysing.
  • Copy FEN and Open in lichess, from the side the diagram is printed from (a diagram printed from Black's side opens from Black's side).

All three use the correction you kept, when there is one: a diagram no model could read can go on the board once you have corrected it.

The More chess actions menu

The ⋯ at the top of the panel gathers the actions on the whole book (they are in the Chess menu too):

  • Chess models… (chapter 2).
  • Make the book's bookmarks… reads the whole book (with its progress and Stop) and first shows the bookmarks it will make: the chapters, the games with a header and the numbered exercises, with the boxes Chapters, Games and Exercises and the bookmark's title. Add the bookmarks makes them in a single step that Edit ▸ Undo takes back.
  • Export the book's review… writes the diagram corrections, the choices made on the book's PGN and the document's games into one file, to take to another computer. The file never carries the PGN's text, nor the PDF's path or name, and the app warns that the games' comments may hold text from the book.
  • Import a book's review… reads the whole file, asks first when it is another book's review (Import anyway · Cancel), merges everything into what this document already has (importing twice duplicates nothing) and says what came in and what could not be used.

Notices in the panel

One notice at a time shows under the panel's header: amber when the piece model is missing (“only diagrams set in a chess font are read”) or when the document changed since its diagrams were detected; red, with an icon, when a model couldn't be loaded or the board couldn't be drawn.

4The diagram editor: correcting a position

A click on a diagram in the list, on Review now or on a diagram's mark on the page opens the editor. With one column, it takes the list's place; with two (from 1,600 pt), it opens in the second. ‹ Diagrams (named Back to Diagrams) and Esc go back to the list, discarding nothing; in a narrow column the button is just its arrow. Read mode and the chess window have the same editor.

What is in the editor

  • Header: ‹ Diagrams, the page and the diagram (Page 3 · diagram 2 of 2; a click shows the diagram on the page) and the arrows ‹ › with a short counter (1/1; the whole text in the tooltip). The arrows walk the diagrams of the chosen filter (with none, those to review).
  • Read | Original (Alt+O): Original shows, in the board's own square and at the same size, the diagram's picture on the page itself, so you can check it square by square without zooming the page. The picture is the one the page view drew: a page not drawn yet says so, with Show it on the page. The choice stays for the next diagram. At the end of the same row, Unconfirmed changes shows while there is a draft, in a place kept for it (the board doesn't jump).
  • Board and palette: the white pieces, the black pieces and the eraser. Doubtful squares are outlined, and each one's second reading is one click away (Empty the square, for instance).
  • To move (White · Black) and Move number:.
  • Details: castling written out, the FEN, Flip board and Restore the automatic reading. A castling right the pieces no longer allow is taken off by itself.
  • The mate a caption asks for: in a “Mate in n” problem, under the board: “Mate in 2: 1.Qf7! (unique)”, the keys of a problem with several solutions, or no mate, with the best try and the defence that refutes it.
  • A fixed footer: the legality chip, the ⋯ menu and the main button. With other diagrams to review it is Confirm and next ▾ Only confirm (the way you used last comes first); with nothing queued, it is Confirm, without ▾. Once confirmed, with nothing changed, it says Confirmed, greyed out until the position changes.

The first time, a tip explains the editor; its × is in its corner. A screen reader hears Diagram board, page 3, diagram 2 and the board as a grid of squares (“e4, White knight”), and To move as a group of two choices.

Example: correcting the diagram with an error

  1. The footer warns that the position is not legal (Black's king is missing). Pick the black king in the palette and click e8. The footer now says Legal position, and the dot on the icon and Unconfirmed changes show there is a draft.
  2. Confirm. The list now says Corrected, and the editor Review done: 1 of 1 checked. The notice at the foot of the page says “Review done: 1 of 1 checked · Diagram 2, page 3: Corrected”, with Undo.

The footer explains any illegal position: a king missing, two kings, kings side by side, the side not to move in check, a pawn on the first or eighth rank, castling the king or rook no longer allows, an en passant square that doesn't fit the pawns…

Confirming and forgetting can be undone

Confirming, Forget the correction and Mark the page as checked are steps of Undo. The notice at the foot of the page carries Undo until your next click or key, and right after, Edit ▸ Undo and Ctrl+Z undo the correction too; undoing a confirmation leaves the position in the editor as unconfirmed changes. Ctrl+Z with the keyboard in the editor never undoes the board's game.

The footer's ⋯ menu

  • Redraw in the PDF…: draws the corrected position over the diagram, in one undo step. The correction is kept first; if it can't be, nothing is drawn and the app says why. The first time, the question Redraw in the PDF? asks you to confirm (Don't show again is remembered).
  • Add lichess link: puts a label with the analysis link under the diagram, from the side the diagram is printed from. Putting the link again after a correction replaces the old label and link with the new ones, in one step; a label someone rewrote, a link drawn over it, or another diagram's link stay.
  • Copy FEN and Open in lichess.
  • Forget the correction, last, apart and in red: goes back to the automatic reading. With unconfirmed changes on top, it asks first (Forget the correction?).

Analyze on the board (the gauge icon in the header) takes the position, as edited, to the board with the engine on.

Drafts, and where the corrections are kept

Unconfirmed changes stay as each diagram's draft while you look at others. When you close the document, quit, export or detect again with drafts pending, the app asks: Confirm correction, Discard changes or Keep editing, with the keyboard on the button that changes nothing. If Confirm correction fails, the question stays open and says why. Keep editing a draft of another document opens that document and that diagram.

The corrections are kept in chess\corrections, one file per book (chapter 2). A correction belongs to one diagram only, the one its box overlaps most, and finds its diagram by the page's content and its place on it, even when pages are inserted before it. Deleting the corrected page doesn't pass the correction to the diagram that moves up into its place: it stays kept, applied to no diagram. With two corrections over one diagram, the one whose page was found by its content wins, then the newer one. While the command line or another window writes in the same folder, Confirm doesn't hold up the window: the change applies at once and is written when the folder is free.

A diagram's correction wins over any new reading: in the list, on the board, in the links and in every export. A build from before 12 November opened alongside reads this build's corrections but never writes over them. In Preferences, Classic diagram editor shows the editor inside the panel, as in older versions, with the same buttons.

When the book is aligned to its PGN and the reading differs from the PGN's position in one or two squares, the editor suggests the PGN's position with Use the PGN's position, as a draft. A correction of yours is never changed by the PGN.

5The Text tab: the book read as chess

The Text tab shows the page on screen read as a chess document: headings, paragraphs, moves and diagrams, named by the number the book prints on the page (Recognised text of page xvii).

The top row and the tab's menu

The top row says the page read and where its text came from (the text layer or the chess OCR), with Read with OCR and the tab's ⋯ menu:

  • Book origin (below);
  • Load the book's PGN…, or Use the PGN that sits beside the PDF (chapter 6);
  • Align the book to the PGN (chapter 6);
  • Export book… (chapter 18);
  • Check with the engine… and, after a check, Divergences (n) (chapter 8);
  • last, in red, Remove the book's PGN.

With the book's PGN, a line under the top row counts the page's moves: Book's PGN on this page: 33 confirmed, 0 read from the PGN, 0 in conflict (0 to review). While the page is being read, the tab says Reading the page… once.

The moves and the state of each

Each paragraph is laid out as one text, its lines evenly spaced. A move's state is told by the colour of its text and by a mark before it, which reads at 4.5:1 in both themes:

×Illegal: it can't be played in the position.

▸A diagram's caption.

Each move's tooltip says its state and its FEN. A move the reader repaired (an old OCR layer's 6.Rel for 6.Re1), or whose printed mark the position doesn't bear out (a capture with nothing to take, a + with no check, a # with no mate), is flagged for review, with what was printed. With the book's PGN, a move the reader couldn't play and the PGN recovered is underlined, and a move in conflict with the PGN is in italics.

Tab stops only at the moves and the diagrams, never at the prose; a screen reader says each move in words, with its state and its link to the PGN.

From the tab to the page and the board

  • A click on a move or a paragraph shows where it is on the page.
  • A click on a diagram opens its editor. If the diagram's page was not detected yet, it is detected first, and the diagram opens as soon as it is found.
  • A double-click on a move, or Open this game on the board in its menu, opens the game on the board at that move. The pages around it (up to four each way) are read too, so a game the book runs on from one page to the next comes whole.

Moves printed on the page

You don't have to go through the Text tab: a double-click on a move printed on the page opens its game on the board at that move (the position after it). The game is the book's PGN's when the move is linked to it, else the page's, read on across the pages around it; the same game, unchanged, is shown again rather than added twice. The word stays selected; Alt+click does the same without selecting.

  • Over a move the Text tab has read, the pointer becomes a hand and the move is underlined in the accent colour. When the pointer rests on it for 0.4 s, a small board with the position after the move shows beside it (below it, or above it near the foot of the window), never covering it; the pointer can move onto the board and it stays. It goes when the pointer leaves both, on a scroll, a zoom or another page, and when a double-click opens the game; Esc closes it, and the keyboard stays where it was.
  • On a page not read yet, the gesture reads the page first, with Reading the page… at the foot of the page, and then opens the move.
  • On a page whose diagrams were not detected yet, a double-click first detects the page's diagrams and puts on the board the one that is there; only where there is no diagram does it go on to the move. On prose, it just selects the word.

It works the same in Read mode.

Book origin

Says where the pages of the open document came from:

ChoiceDoes
Auto (page by page)Trusts each page's text layer unless it looks poor; then it uses the chess OCR.
Born digital (text layer only)Never uses the chess OCR. A page with a poor layer is read as it is, with a note.
Scanned (chess OCR on every page)Reads every page, and every export, with the chess OCR.

A scan that FineReader, Acrobat or Tesseract has already recognised carries their text invisibly over the image, and that layer usually gets figurines and signs wrong. A page whose text is almost all invisible, with images covering half of it or more, is treated as “another program's OCR”, and the Auto mode reads it with the chess OCR. The reading's notes under the page, and the reason not to trust the text layer, are given in the interface's language.

In the chess OCR, a picture on the page (a photograph's halftone, a dusty scan) doesn't stop the reading: the fine dots are left out, and a strip with too many of them is skipped and said in the notes. The book's cipher, what the OCR learned of each page's symbols, is kept per document (chess\cipher): opening a scanned book again uses what earlier readings proved.

Books set in ISChess

Quality Chess and Chess Informant books often set their signs, and sometimes their figurines, in the ISChess font, whose letters stand for something else (O is ±, Q is +−, a is !?). PdfCraft knows the font by its name, so the page is read with the signs and pieces the book prints, without needing the font's file.

How the book is read

  • The text printed between a diagram and the move that settles it goes into the game, as a comment where it stands.
  • The text inside a diagram's box (coordinates, a caption printed in it) is a paragraph of the diagram's own, never a heading; the game goes on after it.
  • Paragraphs are not cut mid-sentence where the layout changes a line's type or weight, and a blank line separates two of them.
  • A square named in a sentence (“an awkward check on h5”, “it does not come to d3”) stays text and is not played. A move in bold is still a move.
  • Labels in capitals (A), B2.1)) separate sibling lines as a) does; text in square brackets with no move goes back to the comment with its brackets.
  • A page in descriptive notation (P-K4, C3AR) says in its notes that this notation is not supported, instead of being left with no games and no explanation.
  • A document holds up to 20,000 games read; the pages go in the book's order, and a game goes on only onto the next page.

6The book's PGN

Many digital books come with a PGN of their own (Chessable and Forward Chess exports…). Aligned to the PDF, that PGN confirms the positions read, recovers moves the reader couldn't play, and gives the exports the whole games, with every variation.

How to load it

  • Offered when you open the book. When a .pgn with the same name sits beside the PDF (also when the names differ only by emoji, symbols, spaces or case, as “📚Book.pdf” and “Book.pgn”), the panel offers to use it: Use or Not now, with the file's name in the text. Without such a PGN, the folder's only .pgn is offered too. The folder is looked through in the background, by names alone: no file is opened, a OneDrive file that isn't downloaded is never offered, and at most 4,096 entries are looked at. The offer shows once per document, and a refusal is remembered.
  • Load the book's PGN… (in the Text tab's ⋯ menu or the Chess menu) picks the file by hand.

The PGN is indexed in the background, without holding up the window, and the Text tab says Indexing the book's PGN… meanwhile. A PGN with a BOM, or in Latin-1 / Windows-1252, is read whole, accents included. PdfCraft indexes up to about 250,000 moves: a larger PGN is still kept, and the notice says that only its first moves are linked to the book.

Aligning the book to the PGN

Align the book to the PGN (in the Text tab's ⋯ menu, where it then becomes Align again, or with Ctrl+K) reads the whole book and links each page to the PGN's games, the Text and Diagrams tabs showing the page it has reached, with a bar and Stop. Stopping writes nothing and leaves the book's link as it was. While the book is aligned, the Text tab goes on reading the page shown. At the end the notice says what it found (The book is linked to its PGN: 84 of 95 moves (6 read from the PGN), 0 in conflict.), with Undo.

What changes with the PGN

  • Diagrams: those that match a position of the PGN become Confirmed by the PGN, with a filter of their own. A diagram keeps its reading as printed and shows the PGN's position; the side the text names (“Position after 5.dxc5”) counts for the description, the FEN and the lichess link of the exports.
  • The Text tab: a move recovered from the PGN is underlined; a move in conflict is in italics, and its menu offers Use the PGN's move or Keep the book's move. In a conflict the tab's text stays the book's; in the exports, the PGN's move wins.
  • The board: a double-click on a linked move opens the PGN's game at that move, with all its variations; In the book looks in the PGN (chapter 9).
  • The exports: every format uses the book's PGN and the document's games (chapter 18).

Your choices in conflicts stay

Each choice keeps the PGN move it was made against. Loading the same PGN again, or another one, keeps your choices; the notice says how many name a move the new PGN doesn't have (“They stay kept for a PGN that has it.”). A choice is found again when a new reading cuts the page into other blocks; one that is not found stays kept, never moves to another move, and the Text tab says how many there are: Choices made on this page not found in its reading (kept): 2.

Where the PGN is kept

The PGN is kept in the chess\reference folder (a copy, not just its path), and it follows the document when you save it under another name. Remove the book's PGN undoes the link, and the file records when the PGN was removed. A file that can't be read is said (“The book's PGN could not be read”), and only the next change sets it aside (….bad). While the command line or another window writes in the same folder, loading, removing or deciding never holds up the window. A build from before 13 November opened alongside doesn't see the PGN loaded in this one.

8Analysing with an engine

Analyze, in the board's header (or Analyze the position in the Chess menu), starts the UCI engine, which analyses the position shown to depth 30. It starts again whenever the position changes, and pauses while the board is out of sight.

  • The lines (1, 2 or 3, under Lines: in the ⋯ menu) show the evaluation for White (+0.40, #3) in the notation chosen for the move list (chapter 17). A click on a move of a line plays the line up to it: moves already in the game are followed, and the others start a variation.
  • The evaluation bar beside the board shows who is better, and the best move is drawn as a blue arrow. In a mated position the bar is all the winner's and the score reads #.
  • The engine is given the game's moves since the last capture or pawn move (up to 100), not just the position, so it sees a repetition or a perpetual check coming. The same position reached by another path is analysed separately.
  • Back on a position already analysed this session (one of the last 64), the lines show at once and the search goes on.
  • The evaluations are kept per document (chess\evals): the best of each position, at depth 10 or more, for the tools (chapter 20). The board doesn't show them yet before its engine reaches them again.

The engine's program

The engine comes from the engine folder beside the program (chapter 2), from PDFCRAFT_CHESS_ENGINE, or from Choose engine…. The board's ⋯ menu says which program it was started (or tried) from, and where. A program that doesn't answer as a UCI engine within 5 s is refused (That program didn't answer as a UCI chess engine.) and ended at once. Turning analysis off, choosing another engine or quitting ends the engine before PdfCraft goes on. With no engine, the panel explains how to get one: Choose engine…, Open stockfishchess.org or Not now. The program, the number of lines and whether analysis is on are remembered.

The engine's preferences

At the end of the Chess section of Preferences, the Chess engine group has Search threads, Hash table and Endgame tables (Syzygy) (Choose folder…, Forget), each on Engine's default until you choose, and remembered for the next session. Once an engine has started in the session, only what it accepts is offered, and the rest is named: This engine does not take: Hash table.

Checking the book with the engine

Check with the engine… (in the Text tab's ⋯ menu and the Chess menu; desktop only) puts the engine to checking what the book says. The dialog asks for the pages and To depth (18) or Seconds each (0.3). Count the positions reads those pages, with a bar, and says before anything starts how many positions the engine will analyse and about how long that takes (Positions to check: 12,000 (≈ 1 h)), leaving out those the kept evaluations already hold at that depth.

Start the check runs in the background, with the row Checking with the engine: position 5 of 12 (… left) and Stop in the Text tab; what the engine has found is kept, and Continue the check (n left), in the ⋯ menu and in the dialog, asks only for the positions still to do. Afterwards, each move of the Text tab shows the evaluation of the position after it, and Divergences (n) (in the tab's ⋯ menu, and as the ≠ n button at its top while there are some) shows the list in the page text's place: the page as the book numbers it, what the book says, what the calculation says, the source (the engine or the mate solver) and Show in PDF; ‹ Text and Esc go back.

The check points out a ! that loses more than 1.5 pawns against the engine's move, a ? that is the engine's own move, an evaluation sign two bands away from the evaluation (a +− below +0.70), a “White to play and win” the evaluation doesn't bear out, an exercise with a second line that also wins, and a mate in n with no mate or with more than one key; below depth 18 nothing is judged. A divergence may be a misreading rather than the book's error: check the diagram and the move on the page before you note an erratum.

Capturing without arming anything

  • Send to PGN ▸, in the bar that shows beside a selection (or in the page's right-click menu), captures the selection in the mode you pick.
  • Ctrl+V with the chess scope on (and no text field in use) captures the clipboard's text. Paste as ▸, in the rail's Capture group menu, picks the mode.
  • Ctrl+C on the page captures too when Also capture what is copied (Ctrl+C) is on in the chip's menu (it is off at first).

The selection grows to whole moves (“20.Nx” becomes “20.Nxf6+”). It is read from the page's characters, not from copied text: a figurine font's pieces are pieces, and a bold move is bold. The language is the document's (as the Text tab read it), never guessed from the selection: Rc7 is a rook in English and a king in Portuguese.

Where a capture goes

  1. The selection's first move decides, by its printed number, at which half-move the text is played.
  2. The candidate lines are the line of the move shown, the line it branched from, and so on out to the main line.
  3. Each possible place is tried on a copy of the game. The place that plays the most moves wins, then the one with the fewest moves that did not play, then the innermost line.
  4. Renumbering is a choice of its own: when the text's numbers fit nowhere, the text can be read from the move shown as if its numbers had been printed for it (12.Raxe1 after 19...Kh7 is played as 20.Raxe1). That never happens without the preview.

The preview: when a capture asks first

A capture goes straight in, as one undo step, only when the whole text was read, it fits one place only, that place is on the line of the move shown, and nothing was demoted or renumbered. Otherwise a preview opens beside the selection and says why:

The buttons are Apply (Enter), Apply and keep the rest as a comment (what was not read is never thrown away) and Cancel (Esc).

Correcting a move that did not play

In the preview, a ✗ chip is a button. It opens, under the chips, Legal moves near it (the reading settled on, the moves an ambiguous reading allows, such as Rd1: Rad1, Rhd1, or the three closest legal moves; none is applied until you click it), a Correction field with the move as printed (the Glyph palette types into it; Enter or Read again reads it again) and, for text from the OCR area, the characters read, the doubtful ones underlined. Each correction rewrites the move where it was printed and reads the whole text again. Nothing goes into the game before Apply.

The OCR area, and collecting for retraining

OCR area (O): on a scanned page, draw a rectangle. The chess OCR reads what is inside it, and the result goes in as a capture, with the preview. Collect corrected characters for retraining (in the chip's menu, optional, desktop only) saves each character of the OCR area that you correct as a 32 × 32 grey picture in a folder you choose, in the layout of ChessVisionOFF's review queue, to train the OCR's glyph model. Nothing is overwritten, at most 2,000 pictures are saved a session, and for an encrypted document the question about your chess work applies. Nothing is trained in the app, and nothing leaves the computer.

Text already captured

Every captured move remembers where it was printed. Underline captured text on the page, in the Capture group's menu, underlines those moves on the page, so you can see what is left to transcribe. A document that doesn't allow copying doesn't allow capturing either. Up to 64 KiB of text goes in one capture.

Diagrams in a game

Diagram here, in a move's menu, shows a small board after the move and is kept in the PGN as $220. Undo

The game has a history of its own, apart from the document's. Every change is a step: a move, a capture, a sign, a comment, a drawing, a structure change, the game's details, a position or a PGN loaded. Walking the moves is not a change. Undo and Redo follow the chess key scope (chapter 11): with the keyboard in the chess panels, they undo in the game; after a click on the page, in the document. Reading and writing PGN

  • A PGN with a BOM, or in Latin-1 / Windows-1252, is read whole, accents included, and the notice says when a file was not UTF-8. Spellings of books and websites (e8Q, a8=q, e.p., …, a ± alone or glued to the move) are read; 1/2 and ½-½ are a draw.
  • A ; comment or a % line holding a { doesn't merge the following games into one; tags on one line or indented are read as tags, and in a tag's value only \\ and \" are escapes (C:\games stays as written). A comment between ) and the next move goes before that move.
  • A comment's commands ([%clk], [%eval], [%csl]…) are kept where they were read and written back there, each whole on one line; the move list shows only the comment's words (chapter 10).
  • The reading notice (“Read with changes: …”) says what was cut, dropped, repaired or ignored, and why a game stopped (an illegal move, an ambiguous move, a game already holding 10,000 moves). A slightly wrong FEN opens repaired; the rest of a line after a move that can't be played becomes a comment where the reading stopped.
  • The Result read is written back as read, * too; a mate or a stalemate that disagrees is counted in the notice. A game of another variant (Chess960, Crazyhouse…) is refused whole, with its name.
  • Every PGN PdfCraft writes comes out of one writer, with lines of at most 79 characters (Copy PGN too); PlyCount follows the moves, and an ECO read is left out once the main line changes before move 10. Write PGN files with a byte order mark (BOM), in Preferences, puts the BOM in.
  • Before writing games read only up to a move that can't be played, Save all games as PGN… names them, with the move where they stopped: Save them as they are or Cancel.

Autosave

Two seconds after the last change, when the document closes and when you quit, each document's games are written to chess\games, one file per document (<hash>.games.json), the same file the command line reads and changes; it doesn't record the PDF's path. A thread of its own writes it, never the window, so a slow disk doesn't hold up the app. When you open the document again, the games come back, with the move shown, the board's side, where each move was printed and the comments before the moves.

  • Each save merges with what the file already has, game by game: games a tool imported or another window added stay and join the list (Chess games restored: 2), and a game deleted here stays deleted there.
  • A diagram opened and not yet touched doesn't count: it gives its place to the next diagram opened and is not saved.
  • Quitting, Close and Close all wait up to 2 s for the saves still pending. When there are games the autosave doesn't keep (or a save that didn't finish), they ask Chess games not saved: Save all games as PGN…, quit or close without saving (Quit without saving, Close without saving), or Cancel.
  • A file written by a newer PdfCraft is only read, with a note in the list (Saved by a newer PdfCraft: these games are only read here.); a new file that can't be read becomes ….games.json.bad at the next save. The .games files of earlier builds (in the recovery folder) are brought in at every start and never renamed or rewritten.
  • Inserting or deleting pages before a game, in the session or between sessions, takes Show in PDF to the right page; if the page is gone, the move has no page.

Protected documents

For an encrypted document, the app asks once, before keeping anything of it: Keep your chess work on this protected document? (Keep them · Don't keep). The answer holds for the corrections, the book's PGN, the games, the character samples, and the reading and cipher kept, all together. Until you answer, and after Don't keep, nothing is written, and the work stays in memory until the document is closed; what is kept is not encrypted. The games of a document that doesn't allow copying are never kept.

PGN for Lichess or ChessBase

Lichess and ChessBase each read the PGN in their own way, and PGN for writes it the way the program you choose keeps it:

  • Standard PGN: every code as read, Black's own sign codes too.
  • Lichess: lichess knows only White's code of a sign that has one for each side, so Black's are written as White's ($37 as $36; the sign stays on Black's move, which says whose it is). A sign lichess doesn't know goes at the start of the move's comment, as its symbol or name ($245 as {⊥}, a diagram as {Diagram}), and the engine's evaluation from Check with the engine… goes as [%eval -1.50] instead of a written comment.
  • ChessBase: Black's codes are written as White's too, since ChessBase drops them when it saves, and a diagram goes as ChessBase's own mark, the comment {[#]}, since ChessBase ignores and drops $220. Every other sign is written as read.

In the PGN — exercises for ChessBase, each exercise's statement starts with ChessBase's training question, so ChessBase's training mode can ask it: {[%tqu "En","White to move (№ 185).","","","d1d8","",10] Page 275 · diagram 1 of 6}. It holds the language (one of ChessBase's tabs: En, De, Fr, Es, It, Ne and Pt; a book in any other language gets En, its text left in its own language), the question (the side to move and the exercise's number), the answer (the solution's first move, as its two squares) and a score of 10; the rest of the statement follows it. ChessBase was only seen asking plain moves, so an exercise whose first move castles or promotes is written without the question, and the window at the end of the export counts them (Without ChessBase's training question: 2). In the question, a " is written as ' and the square brackets as round ones.

What goes into the exported book, and what does not

  • HTML, EPUB, Word, RTF and Markdown are written in the book's language, not in the language of its piece letters: an English book set with figurines comes out in English (“Diagram 1, page 17: White to move”, lang="en").
  • The printed page number, the running head and the text inside a diagram are left out; a game's header stays. A book whose PDF has no page labels is paged, in its EPUB, by the numbers it prints.
  • A move printed wrong that the book's PGN or a document's game plays comes out, in HTML and EPUB, with the position they give, marked, and the text as it was printed.
  • For a screen reader, in HTML and EPUB, signs and figurines are said by their names in the book's language (± is White is clearly better; ♞ on a White move is White knight), in English, Portuguese, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Russian and Polish, and in English for the others; each diagram carries its position in words; each EPUB page has a title, and the contents follow the levels of the headings. In Word and RTF, a sign's name comes in brackets after it.
  • In Word, each diagram goes in twice: as an SVG drawing, which Word 2016 and later (Windows, Mac, the web, phones) show and print sharp at any size, and as a 432-pixel picture of 64 colours for a reader that can't draw the SVG (older Word, some other programs), sharp on screen and a little softer in print. A position the book prints again is stored once. A book of 748 pages and 999 diagrams went from 118.5 MB to 21.2 MB. RTF, Markdown and Anki keep 900-pixel pictures. A Word or RTF file draws at most 30,000 positions (and 1 GB of pictures); a diagram of a new position past that comes out as its FEN, and the export says how many did. No book comes near it.
  • In Markdown, a paragraph printed --- stays text, and a file name with a space or a # doesn't break the links. An EPUB or a Word file that would pass 4 GB or 65,535 files is refused with an error.

The exports' fonts

HTML and EPUB use the fonts of PDFCRAFT_CHESS_TEXT_FONT or, without that variable, those of the fonts folder beside the program, each for what it draws: a figurine font that draws pieces on the letters K Q R B N only for the piece letters of the moves (“Kasparov” in the prose stays a word); a font with the Unicode chess symbols only for those symbols; ISChess for the signs it draws. A WOFF or WOFF2 font, or one that only allows embedding bitmaps, is not embedded.

On this computer

With DiagPDF's fonts imported into dist/app/fonts, the exports write the moves with HastingsFigurine, the first figurine font by name. To use another, leave only that one in the folder, or point to it with PDFCRAFT_CHESS_TEXT_FONT.

Redrawing diagrams and links in the PDF

Redraw in the PDF… (the ⋯ menu of the editor's footer) draws the corrected position over the original diagram, with the pieces chosen for Diagrams, in one undo step, and only that page is read again. Add lichess link puts a label with the analysis link under the diagram. The label and link PdfCraft puts carry a mark of their own: putting them again replaces only a pair with that mark, of the same diagram. These two actions change the PDF and show Undo in their notice; corrections alone never change the PDF. Make the book's bookmarks… (chapter 3) changes the PDF too, in one undo step.

Known limits

  • More than four columns are read as four at most. A table set in columns (an index of games) is read column by column, like text.
  • On a scanned page, a reading with c and e swapped (a scan loses the bar of the e) is tried when the printed move doesn't play. The move is played only when exactly one reading is legal, and it is flagged for review.
  • A PGN file keeps the comment before a move only where it has a place for it (the autosave always keeps it).
  • The game's undo history and the games list's don't carry from one session to the next; importing a review is not undone by Undo.
  • The progress bar doesn't estimate the time left yet. Reading a page with OCR can't be stopped once it has started.
  • In the book doesn't count the moves the reading found in the text that no PGN has, and gives the page where a game starts, not the move's own page.
  • A game the command line, another window or an imported review changed or deleted while the document is open shows so only when you open the document again (games added come in at once).
  • Making the bookmarks again adds another tree. The list of errata exists only through the tools.
  • From the app, the exercise sheet is A4 only (Letter through the tool). An exercise's caption and statement don't yet say the goal the book prints (“and win”). Anki's pictures are copied into collection.media by hand.
  • The PGNs written for Lichess and ChessBase follow what each program was seen to keep, but no such file has yet been imported into lichess or ChessBase, and the exercises with ChessBase's training question have not yet been opened in ChessBase's training. The list of signs lichess knows is the published one, not checked on the site.
  • The Word file was checked from the inside, not opened in Word, LibreOffice or Google Docs.
  • An export's notes and the reason a sheet was refused are in English.
  • In an encrypted book, the Text tab's reading is not kept yet, even after Keep them. No button of the app clears the reading kept; only the chess_cache_clear tool does.
  • In a folder of more than 4,096 entries (such as Downloads), a PGN with the PDF's name beyond the first 4,096 is not offered: use Load the book's PGN….
  • If PdfCraft closes suddenly with the engine on, the engine may stay open (Stockfish quits by itself).
  • Piece sets by other authors draw gradients flat, in their first colour. The DG cuts have frames and labels of their own, which are read but not drawn.
